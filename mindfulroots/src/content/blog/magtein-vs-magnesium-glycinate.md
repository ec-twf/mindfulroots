If you are weighing "Magtein" against magnesium glycinate, start with the one fact every vendor page skips: both are magnesium. Neither is stronger overall. Magtein is a branded magnesium L-threonate studied for memory and for raising magnesium inside the brain, and it costs noticeably more. Magnesium glycinate is the well-absorbed, gentle, cheaper form most people use for sleep, calm and correcting a shortfall. Choose Magtein for a specific cognition goal; choose glycinate for sleep, everyday stress or general repletion. Our [magnesium glycinate page](/products/magnesium-glycinate/) covers the value option in full.

Because the two forms are aimed at different jobs, forcing a single winner would mislead. The honest framing is per goal: what are you actually trying to change, and does the evidence for that form support it.

## Two forms, two destinations

Magnesium glycinate is magnesium bound to two molecules of glycine, an amino acid that is itself mildly calming. That pairing is easy on the gut and well absorbed, which is why glycinate is the default for sleep and anxiety support and for topping up a body-wide deficiency. The brain is not its target; it simply raises your overall magnesium status, and low status alone can worsen mood and sleep.

Magtein is a trademarked magnesium L-threonate, magnesium joined to threonic acid, developed specifically to reach the brain. The interest traces to animal work: [Slutsky and colleagues (2010)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20152124/), publishing in *Neuron*, reported that magnesium L-threonate raised magnesium in the brains of rats and improved learning and memory measures, where other forms did not shift brain magnesium as readily. That mechanism, not a larger dose, is the entire case for the premium.

## What the human evidence shows

Temper the animal excitement with what has actually been tested in people, which is little. The main human study, [Liu and colleagues (2016)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26519439/), gave magnesium L-threonate to older adults with self-reported cognitive problems and reported improvement on cognitive measures over 12 weeks. It was small and industry-connected, so it is a promising signal rather than settled proof. For glycinate's home turf, magnesium's role in sleep and calm, a placebo-controlled trial by [Abbasi and colleagues (2012)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23853635/) found magnesium improved insomnia measures in older adults, and a review by [Boyle and colleagues (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28445426/) judged the anxiety evidence promising but limited. The [NIH magnesium fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/) is the baseline reference for dosing and safety across all forms.

## What to look for in a magnesium L-threonate supplement for cognition

If a cognition goal is what sends you to threonate, buy the studied thing. First, confirm it names **Magtein** by the trademark, since that is the material used in the human trial. Second, read past the marketing weight: the label should state the **elemental magnesium** per serving as well as the L-threonate amount, and the studied intake is about **1.5 to 2 grams of magnesium L-threonate a day**, usually split into two or three doses. Third, insist on **third-party testing** for identity and contaminants. If instead your aim is sleep, everyday stress, or fixing a deficiency, a threonate product is an expensive route and glycinate is the better value; our [magnesium glycinate page](/products/magnesium-glycinate/) and the [sleep guide](/guides/sleep/) cover that path.

## When the premium is not worth it

Magtein is not worth the extra unless brain-specific cognition is genuinely your goal. If you mainly want better sleep, calmer evenings, or to correct a magnesium shortfall, glycinate delivers as much or more elemental magnesium for a fraction of the price, with broader evidence behind those uses. Threonate also asks you to swallow a larger daily powder load for less actual magnesium, so using it for general repletion means paying more to absorb less. Reserve the premium for the one job the research points to, and let the cheaper form handle everything else. Where the goal is not cognition, the branded form buys you nothing the body will notice.

## Cautions

Magnesium in either form is well tolerated by most adults, with loose stools the first sign of too much. Total elemental magnesium from all supplements is what matters, so if you stack glycinate and Magtein, keep the combined amount within the usual daily range. People with reduced kidney function should not supplement magnesium without medical advice. Magnesium can bind some antibiotics and thyroid medication, so separate them by a few hours. If you are pregnant, take prescription medicine, or have a health condition, talk to your prescriber first. This article is educational support, not individual medical advice.
