For most people the practical answer is the evening, taken with a small amount of food. Evening dosing leans into serotonin's role in winding down toward sleep, and a little food blunts the nausea that is 5-HTP's most common side effect. The main exception is daytime mood support, where a smaller split dose earlier in the day can suit better. Consistency matters more than the exact clock time.

Before the detail, one non-negotiable: if you take any serotonergic medication, timing is not the issue, safety is, and 5-HTP needs a prescriber's sign-off. Our [5-HTP hub](/products/5-htp/) covers that in full.

## The short answer

Take 5-HTP in the **evening, with a light snack**, if your goal is sleep or general calm. Take a **smaller dose earlier in the day**, or split it morning and evening, if daytime mood is the target. Start low, keep the total modest, and give any routine a few weeks rather than judging it overnight. Pick a time you can actually stick to, because regularity beats precision here.

## Why the evening tends to fit

5-HTP (5-hydroxytryptophan) is one step from serotonin, and serotonin is itself the raw material your body uses to make melatonin, the hormone that signals sleep. That pathway is the reason evening dosing is the common default: supporting serotonin as the day ends sits naturally alongside the wind-down toward bed. The role of 5-HTP as a serotonin precursor, and its ability to cross into the brain where tryptophan competes less, is reviewed in detail by [Birdsall, *Alternative Medicine Review*, 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9727088/).

This is a rationale, not a proven sleep protocol. Evidence for 5-HTP specifically as a sleep aid is limited, so treat the evening timing as a sensible fit rather than a guarantee.

## Why food helps

The most reliable reason to mind your timing is tolerability. Nausea and mild stomach upset are the side effects people most often report with 5-HTP, and they tend to show up early and at higher doses. A review of 5-HTP supplementation notes that gastrointestinal effects are dose-related and a common reason people stop ([Turner et al., *Pharmacology & Therapeutics*, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pharmthera.2005.06.004)). Taking the dose with a small amount of food, and starting at the low end, is the simplest way to keep that in check while your body adjusts.

## Daytime use and splitting the dose

If you are using 5-HTP for mood during the day rather than for sleep, a single evening dose may not match when you want the support. In that case a smaller amount earlier, or a split across morning and evening, is a reasonable approach. The clinical trials behind 5-HTP for low mood generally used divided daily doses rather than one large hit ([Shaw, Turner & Del Mar, *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews*, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003198)), which is consistent with splitting as a way to keep each dose gentle. Keep the total within what you and your clinician have agreed, however you divide it.

## What to look for in a 5-HTP supplement for an evening routine

If an evening wind-down is your use case, the label choices that matter are modest and specific:

- **A per-capsule dose that keeps your total low**, often 50–100 mg, so you can start small and adjust.
- **Source:** *Griffonia simplicifolia* seed extract, the standard natural source.
- **Vitamin B6** in the formula is a plus, since it is the cofactor for converting 5-HTP to serotonin.
- **Third-party testing**, and no added stimulants or other serotonergic ingredients that would be out of place before bed.

For a calming evening routine, a lower per-capsule dose you take consistently beats a high-strength bottle you have to break up. Look for a tested 50–100 mg Griffonia-sourced capsule rather than the highest milligram count on the shelf.

## Cautions and interactions

Timing changes nothing about 5-HTP's central safety issue. Taken with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, triptans, tramadol or other serotonergic drugs, 5-HTP can contribute to serotonin syndrome, a rare but serious reaction, no matter what time of day you take it. Do not combine 5-HTP with any such medication, and speak to your prescriber before starting it at all. Stop and seek care if you notice agitation, a racing heart, sweating, shivering or confusion after dosing.

If low mood or poor sleep is persistent, 5-HTP is support, not a cure, and ongoing problems deserve a clinician's attention. See our [get help](/get-help/) page.
