5-HTP works on two clocks. A single dose peaks in the blood within a few hours, and some people notice a calming or drowsy effect the same day, which is why it is popular for sleep. A real change in mood is slower and builds over roughly two to six weeks of daily use, which is the window the trials that reported a benefit generally used. So if you are taking it for low mood, plan for weeks, not days, and give it a fair, consistent trial. One caution comes first, before anything else: because 5-HTP raises serotonin, it must never be combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, or other serotonergic medicines, because of the risk of serotonin syndrome.

## Why the same-day effect is not the mood effect

5-HTP is a direct precursor to serotonin: the body converts it in one step, which is why a dose can register quickly. That fast step explains the same-day calming or sedating feeling many people report, and why an evening dose is common for sleep. Mood is different. Lifting low mood through the serotonin system is not a matter of a single dose but of a sustained change, and that develops over weeks, much as it does with conventional serotonergic treatments. So the quick effect you might feel on day one is not a preview of the mood benefit; it is a separate, more immediate response. Judging the mood effect too early is the most common mistake, and it usually leads people to quit before the relevant timeline has even started.

## What the evidence shows

The studies that looked at mood measured it over weeks. A [2002 Cochrane review by Shaw, Turner, and Del Mar](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003198) concluded that 5-HTP and tryptophan appeared better than placebo for depression in the available trials, while stressing the evidence base was small and of limited quality. A [2013 comparison by Jangid and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajp.2012.09.014) ran 5-HTP against fluoxetine over several weeks and found both groups improved on depression scores. On the pharmacology, a [2006 review by Turner and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pharmthera.2005.06.004) describes 5-HTP's rapid absorption and short half-life, which is why the same-day feel and the multi-week mood timeline can both be true. Across this work the picture is consistent: mood effects are gradual and modest, the trials are small, and 5-HTP is not a same-day mood fix.

## What to look for in a 5-HTP supplement for low mood

Because the mood effect is slow and the tolerability varies, start low and read the label carefully. Choose a product that states the 5-HTP dose per serving in milligrams, usually standardised from *Griffonia simplicifolia* seed extract, and that offers a low starting dose so you can build up gradually rather than jumping to a high amount. Nausea is the most common early side effect, so a divided-dose or enteric-coated format can help. Check for third-party testing for identity and contaminants. Avoid blends that combine 5-HTP with other serotonergic ingredients, which muddies both the dose and the safety picture. ## Cautions

This is the most important section. Never combine 5-HTP with an SSRI, SNRI, MAOI, triptan, tramadol, or any other serotonergic medicine, because raising serotonin on top of these drugs can cause serotonin syndrome, a potentially serious reaction. Stop and seek urgent help if you develop agitation, a fast heartbeat, high fever, muscle rigidity, or confusion. Even on its own, start at a low dose to gauge tolerability, and be cautious if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, or have a medical condition. 5-HTP supports mood; it does not treat depression or any diagnosed condition. Talk to your prescriber before starting it, especially if you take any medication, and if you are struggling, see [/get-help/](/get-help/).
