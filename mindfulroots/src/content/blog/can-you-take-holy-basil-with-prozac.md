Holy basil, or tulsi (*Ocimum sanctum*), is an adaptogenic herb from Ayurveda used to support the body's response to stress. Prozac (fluoxetine) is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor, an SSRI prescribed for depression and anxiety that works by raising serotonin activity in the brain. Can you take them together? There is no well-documented, proven interaction, but there is a credible theoretical one, which makes this a question to bring to your prescriber rather than settle yourself. The concern sits on two fronts: serotonin and bleeding.

Because holy basil's own evidence is limited and this pairing carries real uncertainty, our better-studied [ashwagandha hub](/products/ashwagandha/) is a more useful reference if you are exploring adaptogens for mood, though it comes with its own medication cautions. This post explains why the Prozac combination deserves caution.

## The serotonin concern

SSRIs like fluoxetine increase serotonin signalling, which is how they support mood. Serotonin syndrome is what happens when that signalling is pushed too far, usually by stacking two or more serotonin-raising agents, and it can range from mild agitation and tremor to a medical emergency ([Boyer & Shannon, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMra041867)). Holy basil is not a drug, but preclinical studies have reported antidepressant-like and mild serotonergic activity for *Ocimum sanctum* extracts. That is a long way from proving it raises serotonin meaningfully in people, yet it is enough that combining it with an SSRI is not something to treat casually.

The honest framing is uncertainty, not alarm. No robust human case series ties holy basil to serotonin syndrome. But the direction of its known activity overlaps with the drug's, and when the downside is a serious reaction, a theoretical overlap is worth respecting.

## The bleeding concern

The second issue is less discussed but concrete. SSRIs, fluoxetine included, are associated with an increased bleeding tendency because serotonin plays a role in platelet function. Holy basil has shown antiplatelet activity in laboratory and animal studies, a caution echoed by [Memorial Sloan Kettering's herb reference](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/holy-basil). Two agents that each nudge clotting downward could add up, which becomes most relevant around surgery or alongside blood thinners or regular NSAID use.

## What the safety evidence actually shows

A 2017 systematic review of tulsi in humans pooled 24 studies and found the herb generally well tolerated across stress and metabolic endpoints, but it also flagged that trials were small and interaction data in people were essentially absent ([Jamshidi & Cohen, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1155/2017/9217567)). In other words, the reassurance about tolerability comes from studies of holy basil used *alone*, not combined with antidepressants. The [NIH's complementary health resources](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/dietary-and-herbal-supplements) make the same general point about herb-drug combinations: absence of documented harm is not the same as proven safety, especially with prescription medication.

## The bottom line

Do not combine holy basil with Prozac on your own, and do not stop or adjust your Prozac to make room for a supplement. The interaction is theoretical rather than proven, but it touches two serious mechanisms, serotonin and bleeding, and the human data to rule it out simply do not exist.
