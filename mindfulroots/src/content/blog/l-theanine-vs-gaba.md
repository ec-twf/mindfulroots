If your goal is daytime calm without feeling sedated, L-theanine is the more dependable of the two. It reliably reaches the brain and has human trials linking it to lower stress and steadier attention. Oral GABA is harder to back: because it may not cross the blood-brain barrier well as a supplement, it is unclear whether a GABA pill does much directly in the brain at all.

Both are sold for the same feeling, so the real question is which one actually gets where it needs to go. You can see the spec on the [L-theanine page](/products/l-theanine/), and the [anxiety guide](/guides/anxiety/) sets it among other calming options.

## The blood-brain barrier is the whole argument

GABA is the brain's primary inhibitory neurotransmitter, the chemical that quietens neural activity, so on paper swallowing GABA sounds like the obvious calm supplement. The catch is delivery. The blood-brain barrier tightly controls what gets into the brain, and there is real doubt about whether oral GABA crosses it in useful amounts. Where people do report calming effects, some researchers suspect it acts on the gut's nervous system, which then signals the brain, rather than acting in the brain directly.

L-theanine, an amino acid from tea, does not have that problem. It is absorbed and does cross into the brain, where it is thought to nudge alpha brainwave activity and modestly influence GABA and glutamate signalling. Same destination, but only one of the two has a clear route there.

## What the evidence actually shows

L-theanine has the stronger human record. A review of its effects on mood found it can promote relaxation without sedation ([Nobre et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18296328/)), and a four-week randomised trial in healthy adults reported reductions in stress-related symptoms and some cognitive gains ([Hidese et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11102362)). The picture is not spectacular, but it is consistent and it is human.

GABA's evidence is thinner and turns on that delivery question. A review of GABA as a food supplement concluded that the case for meaningful direct brain effects from oral GABA is limited, and that its blood-brain barrier crossing remains uncertain ([Boonstra et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01520)). Some small studies report relaxation or stress markers shifting, but the mechanism is unresolved, so it is hard to be confident about what a GABA capsule is really doing.

## Which L-theanine supplement to buy for calm

Because L-theanine is a single, well-defined amino acid, choosing well is mostly about dose and purity. Look for a product that states the L-theanine dose per capsule, most commonly 100 to 200 mg, since that is the range used in calm and focus studies. A single-ingredient capsule is easier to judge than a proprietary "calm blend" that hides how much of anything you are getting. Third-party testing for identity and contaminants is worth having, and Suntheanine is a well-characterised branded form if you want a known quantity rather than a generic. If you drink coffee, L-theanine pairs naturally with caffeine to take the edge off jitters. ## Cautions

L-theanine is well tolerated for most people, and GABA supplements are generally regarded as low-risk, but low-risk is not the same as proven effective. Both can add to the effect of sedatives, and GABA may lower blood pressure, so if you take medication for blood pressure, anxiety or sleep, clear either one with your prescriber first. Neither replaces care for a diagnosed condition; they support a calmer routine, nothing more.
