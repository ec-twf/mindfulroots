Take L-theanine when you want to feel it. There is no universally better time, because its effect comes on within about an hour and fades over a few hours rather than building up from one day to the next. Mornings suit calm, steady focus and smoothing out coffee jitters; evenings suit winding down before bed. Plenty of people who dose twice do both. Our [L-theanine hub](/products/l-theanine/) covers typical doses and what to check on the label.

## The short answer

Pick the time by your **goal**, not the clock. Use a **morning** dose for relaxed focus during the day or to soften the jitters from caffeine. Use an **evening** dose, roughly 30 to 60 minutes before bed, to quiet a busy mind as you wind down. If you want both, morning and night is a reasonable split. Because L-theanine does not accumulate, you can change your timing freely without a "washout" period.

## Why timing follows the effect, not a rule

L-theanine is an amino acid found naturally in tea, and the state people describe on it is relaxed alertness rather than sedation. That profile is the reason it does not have a single correct time of day. Pharmacokinetic work shows L-theanine is absorbed quickly, with blood levels peaking within roughly the first hour and then clearing over the next several hours ([Scheid et al., *The Journal of Nutrition*, 2012](https://doi.org/10.3945/jn.112.166371)). A dose is therefore a "now" tool: it does its work while it is present and is largely gone by the opposite end of the day, which is why a morning capsule and an evening capsule can serve completely different goals.

## What the morning case looks like

The daytime argument rests on calm without drowsiness. In a randomised controlled trial in healthy adults, four weeks of L-theanine was associated with lower stress-related scores and some gains on cognitive measures such as verbal fluency and executive function ([Hidese et al., *Nutrients*, 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11102362)). It is also the classic partner for caffeine: taken together, L-theanine is reported to smooth the jittery edge of coffee while focus holds. If your goal is to get through a demanding morning feeling steady rather than wired, that is when to take it.

## What the evening case looks like

The night-time argument is about downshifting. A controlled study found L-theanine reduced physiological and psychological markers of the stress response under a demanding task ([Kimura et al., *Biological Psychology*, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2006.06.006)), and a systematic review of green-tea L-theanine concluded it can help people manage stress and anxiety at typical doses while cautioning that the overall evidence base is still modest ([Williams et al., *Plant Foods for Human Nutrition*, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11130-019-00771-5)). For sleep specifically, read that as support for winding down, not a sedative. Taken 30 to 60 minutes before bed, it helps quiet a racing mind rather than knocking you out.

## What to look for in an L-theanine supplement for an evening wind-down

If evenings are your use case, the label choices that matter are few and clear:

- **Single-ingredient L-theanine**, with the milligrams per capsule stated, commonly 100 to 200 mg.
- **No caffeine or stimulants** in the formula, which is the key point for night-time use.
- **Third-party testing** for identity and purity.
- **Form:** the branded **Suntheanine** is widely used, but generic L-theanine is the same molecule, so do not pay a premium expecting a different compound.

A caffeine-free capsule you can take consistently before bed beats a focus "stack" that hides a stimulant. Look for a tested 100 to 200 mg single-ingredient capsule with nothing stimulating added.

## Cautions and interactions

L-theanine is very well tolerated, and timing does not change that. If you take blood-pressure medication, mention L-theanine to your doctor, as it may have a mild lowering effect. It is not a treatment for a diagnosed anxiety or sleep disorder, and persistent problems deserve a clinician's attention rather than a supplement alone. See our [get help](/get-help/) page.
