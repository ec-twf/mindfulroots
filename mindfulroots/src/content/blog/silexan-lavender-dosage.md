Silexan is a patented oral preparation of lavender (*Lavandula angustifolia*) oil, standardised so that each capsule delivers a consistent amount of the plant's two active components, linalool and linalyl acetate. Unlike an aromatherapy oil meant for a diffuser, this is a swallowed softgel developed and tested for easing everyday anxiety symptoms. The dose that carries the most evidence is **80 mg once daily**, with some trials of stronger symptoms using 160 mg per day. Below is what the research actually tested, when to take it, and how it compares with other calming options.

## The dose the trials actually used

Almost all of the clinical work on Silexan settled on a single daily capsule. A double-blind trial by [Kasper and colleagues (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1097/YIC.0b013e32833b3242) tested 80 mg per day against placebo in adults with subsyndromal anxiety and reported meaningful reductions in restlessness and disturbed sleep over ten weeks. A later comparison by [Kasper and colleagues (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1461145714000017) put 160 mg of Silexan up against a low dose of the antidepressant paroxetine and against placebo in generalised anxiety, again taken once daily.

Pulling those studies together, a [meta-analysis by Generoso and colleagues (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1097/JCP.0000000000000629) concluded that Silexan outperformed placebo for anxiety symptoms across the trials, with 80 mg as the common dose and 160 mg reserved for more pronounced presentations. The practical read is simple: 80 mg once a day is the evidence-backed starting dose, and the jump to 160 mg belongs in a clinician's hands.

## Timing and how long to give it

Silexan is taken once daily, and many people take it in the morning with water. The main reason is comfort rather than effect: like several oral botanical oils, it can cause a mild lavender-scented burp, which a morning dose and a full glass of water tend to soften. Splitting the capsule is not the practice the studies followed, so a single daily dose is the pattern to copy.

This is not a supplement that works the moment you swallow it. The trials measured change over two to six weeks, with benefits building gradually rather than arriving on the first evening. If you are looking for immediate calm before a specific event, Silexan is the wrong tool. Its value shows up when it is taken steadily, day after day, for at least a few weeks. Set that expectation before you start and you are less likely to abandon it too early or reach for a higher dose out of impatience.

## Silexan versus generic lavender

A recurring mistake is treating any lavender capsule as interchangeable with Silexan. It is not. The [Examine summary of lavender](https://examine.com/supplements/lavender/) notes that the anxiety evidence rests specifically on this standardised oral oil, not on aromatherapy or unstandardised capsules. Generic products vary widely in how much linalool and linalyl acetate they contain, and the [NCCIH overview of lavender](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/lavender) is careful to say that most claims for other forms are not well supported. If your aim is to reproduce the research, matching the standardisation is what counts.

## What to look for in a lavender oil supplement for anxiety

If you decide to try lavender, the label is where the useful information lives. Prioritise an **oral preparation, not an essential oil sold for diffusing**, since only swallowed, standardised oil was tested. Check that it states the **80 mg standardised dose** and names the **linalool and linalyl acetate content**, the two markers that define the studied product. Third-party testing for purity matters more here than any calming imagery on the box. ## Cautions and interactions

Silexan is generally well tolerated, with lavender-scented burping the most common report. It may add to the effect of sedatives, and its safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding has not been established, so those groups should avoid it. Because anxiety can have medical causes and can overlap with other conditions, ongoing or severe symptoms deserve a proper assessment rather than self-treatment. Talk to your prescriber or pharmacist before starting Silexan if you take any medication, and before moving above 80 mg per day.
