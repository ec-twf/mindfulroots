Take L-theanine whenever you want the calm, because it works within about an hour and fades over a few hours rather than building up day to day. You do not need to take it with food. It suits a consistent daily time if your goal is steady, background calm, and it works just as well taken on demand, 30 to 60 minutes before a stressful moment. Our [L-theanine hub](/products/l-theanine/) covers typical doses and what to check on the label.

## The short answer

Time it to your **goal**. For general, steady calm, pick a **consistent daily time** and keep to it: morning for relaxed focus, evening to wind down, or both if you dose twice. For a specific stressful event, take it **30 to 60 minutes beforehand**. Food is **optional**: L-theanine absorbs fine on an empty stomach, so only add a snack if a larger dose feels unsettling. Because it clears within a few hours, you can change your timing whenever you like.

## Why there is no single "right" time

L-theanine is an amino acid from tea, and the state it supports is relaxed alertness rather than sedation. That is why, unlike a sleep hormone, it has no fixed slot in the day. Pharmacokinetic work shows L-theanine is absorbed quickly, with blood levels peaking within roughly the first hour and clearing over the following hours ([Scheid et al., *The Journal of Nutrition*, 2012](https://doi.org/10.3945/jn.112.166371)). That short, clean profile is what makes timing a question of *when you want the effect* rather than *when the supplement works*. A morning dose is largely gone by night, and vice versa, so you choose the moment.

## For steady, everyday calm: pick a time and keep it

If your goal is lower day-to-day tension rather than coverage for one event, consistency is the useful habit. Reviews of L-theanine describe effects on self-reported relaxation and mental state at typical doses ([Nobre, Rao & Owen, *Asia Pacific Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18296328/)), and a four-week randomised trial in healthy adults linked daily L-theanine to lower stress-related scores and some cognitive gains ([Hidese et al., *Nutrients*, 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11102362)). None of that depends on a precise hour. Anchoring the dose to something you already do every day, like your morning coffee or brushing your teeth at night, is the simplest way to keep it regular.

## For a specific moment: take it on demand

L-theanine's quick onset also makes it a good "before the thing" option. In a controlled study of anticipatory anxiety, a single dose of L-theanine blunted the rise in anxiety ahead of a stressful task ([Lu et al., *Human Psychopharmacology*, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1002/hup.611)). Taken 30 to 60 minutes before a presentation, exam or social situation, it has time to reach peak levels while the event is still ahead of you. This is where timing genuinely matters: too early and it may fade, too late and it has not landed yet.

## Does food change anything?

Not meaningfully. L-theanine is well absorbed with or without a meal and is very well tolerated, so stomach upset is uncommon. The only practical reason to pair it with food is if a larger single dose feels slightly unsettling, in which case a light snack smooths it over. Otherwise, take it whenever your timing goal calls for, meal or no meal.

## Which L-theanine supplement to buy for everyday use

For daily use, the label details that matter are few and specific:

- **Single-ingredient L-theanine**, with milligrams per capsule stated, commonly 100 to 200 mg.
- **Third-party testing** for identity and purity.
- **No caffeine or stimulants** unless you specifically want a daytime focus stack, since a plain capsule is more flexible to dose morning or night.
- **Form:** the branded **Suntheanine** is widely used, but generic L-theanine is the same molecule, so there is no need to pay extra expecting a different compound.

A plain, tested capsule you can dose flexibly beats a fixed-combination product when the goal is everyday calm. Look for a 100 to 200 mg single-ingredient capsule you can take at whatever time your routine fits.

## Cautions and interactions

L-theanine has a strong safety record, and timing does not change that. If you take blood-pressure medication, mention L-theanine to your doctor, as it may have a mild lowering effect. It is support for stress and focus, not a treatment for a diagnosed anxiety or sleep disorder, and persistent difficulties deserve a clinician's attention. See our [get help](/get-help/) page.
