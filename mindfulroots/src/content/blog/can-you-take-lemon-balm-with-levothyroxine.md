Lemon balm (*Melissa officinalis*) is a lemon-scented herb in the mint family, taken as a tea or extract to ease tension and support calm before sleep. Levothyroxine is a synthetic form of the thyroid hormone thyroxine, prescribed to replace what an underactive thyroid cannot make. The question is whether the calming herb is safe alongside a medication whose whole job is to hold your thyroid hormone at a precise level. The honest answer is that no strong human study has settled it, and the caution that does exist rests on laboratory findings worth understanding before you combine them.

If you take levothyroxine and mainly want something calming, it helps to know there are thyroid-neutral options. Minerals such as [magnesium glycinate](/products/magnesium-glycinate/) support the same wind-down without the thyroid questions attached to some herbs, and our [stress guide](/guides/stress/) walks through how these supports compare.

## The theoretical thyroid concern

The reason clinicians raise an eyebrow here traces back to older laboratory work. In a study by [Auf'mkolk and colleagues (1985)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2981743/), extracts of lemon balm and related mint-family plants were able to block thyroid-stimulating hormone from binding its receptor and to interfere with the antibodies that drive Graves' disease. That is a test-tube result, not a measurement in people on levothyroxine, so it cannot tell you how much herb, if any, would shift a real thyroid panel. What it does explain is why lemon balm carries a thyroid caution at all.

The [Memorial Sloan Kettering herb monograph on lemon balm](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/lemon-balm) lists a theoretical interaction with thyroid medication for the same reason, and the independent summary at [Examine](https://examine.com/supplements/lemon-balm/) rates lemon balm's calming evidence as promising but modest in size. Neither claims a proven danger; both flag the plausible overlap.

## Why timing and dose matter

There are two separate issues to keep apart. The first is the thyroid-signalling question above. The second is plainer: levothyroxine absorption is notoriously easy to disturb. It is taken on an empty stomach precisely because food, coffee, calcium, iron, and fibre can all blunt how much reaches your blood. Any supplement swallowed in that same window risks the same effect, regardless of its own biology.

So even if your prescriber is comfortable with lemon balm, the sensible pattern is to take your levothyroxine first thing, wait the usual gap, and keep the herb well away from it, typically by at least four hours. That protects your dose from an absorption problem while the thyroid-signalling question is monitored separately through your regular blood tests.

## Talk to your prescriber first

This is a genuine your-health question, not a self-experiment. Because levothyroxine is dosed to a narrow target and adjusted by blood tests, anything with even a theoretical thyroid effect belongs in a conversation with the clinician who manages that dose. Tell them what you want to take, ask whether to add lemon balm at all, and let your usual thyroid monitoring catch any drift. Do not stop or change your levothyroxine on your own.
