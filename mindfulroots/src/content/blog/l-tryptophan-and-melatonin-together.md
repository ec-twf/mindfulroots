L-tryptophan is an essential amino acid your body uses as the starting material to build serotonin and, downstream of that, melatonin. Melatonin is the hormone your pineal gland releases in the dark to signal night. Can you take the two together? For most healthy adults the answer is yes, because they act at different points on the same chain rather than fighting each other. The sensible cautions are about additive drowsiness and, more seriously, tryptophan's overlap with serotonin medication, not a chemical clash between the supplements themselves.

If your real interest is the serotonin-precursor side of sleep, our [5-HTP hub](/products/5-htp/) is worth reading first, since 5-HTP is the step between tryptophan and serotonin and the form most sleep and mood formulas actually use. This post stays on the pairing question.

## How the pathway fits together

Both supplements feed one route. Tryptophan crosses into the brain, gets converted to 5-HTP, then to serotonin, and at night the serotonin is converted onward to melatonin. Supplemental melatonin simply drops the finished hormone into your system without waiting on that conversion.

Because tryptophan sits upstream and melatonin sits at the end, taking them together tops up the raw material and adds the signal at once. There is no known mechanism by which one blocks the other. What they do share is a direction of effect: both push you toward sleep, so the combined sedative pull can be stronger than either alone. The [NCCIH melatonin overview](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/melatonin-what-you-need-to-know) notes that melatonin is generally used at low doses for short periods, and stacking a precursor on top does not change that low-dose logic.

## What the evidence actually shows

There is no trial of the exact tryptophan-plus-melatonin combination, so the honest read is that we lean on the evidence for each on its own. A 2013 meta-analysis found melatonin modestly shortened the time to fall asleep and lengthened total sleep in people with primary sleep problems ([Ferracioli-Oda et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0063773)). For the precursor side, a Cochrane review of tryptophan and 5-HTP for low mood found a signal of benefit but flagged that trial quality was mixed ([Shaw et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003198)). Neither body of work suggests the two interfere; both suggest keeping doses modest and expectations realistic.

## What to look for in a 5-HTP supplement to take with melatonin

If the reason you are reaching for tryptophan is serotonin support for sleep, most people are better served by 5-HTP, the more direct precursor that crosses into the brain readily. A few criteria separate a product worth buying from marketing. Check the **stated 5-HTP dose per capsule** (50 to 100 mg is typical) rather than a proprietary blend that hides the amount. Prefer 5-HTP from **Griffonia simplicifolia** seed, the standard source, named plainly on the label. Look for **third-party testing** (USP, NSF, or an independent certificate of analysis), since precursor supplements have had contamination scares in the past. If you plan to add melatonin, keep it to a **low separate dose** rather than a high-dose combined "sleep" blend you cannot titrate. ## Cautions and interactions

The one that matters most is serotonin syndrome. L-tryptophan and 5-HTP raise serotonin activity, and combining any serotonin-raising agent with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, triptans, tramadol, or other serotonergic drugs can cause a dangerous build-up of serotonin. Warning signs include agitation, a racing heart, sweating, tremor, and confusion; the classic clinical review by [Boyer and Shannon (2005)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15784664/) describes the picture in full. Beyond that, both supplements can cause daytime drowsiness, so avoid driving until you know how the pair affects you, and melatonin can interact with blood thinners and blood-pressure medication. These support sleep; they do not treat a diagnosed sleep or mood disorder.

If you take any prescription medication, especially an antidepressant, talk to your prescriber before combining L-tryptophan and melatonin. This is a medical decision, not a self-experiment.
