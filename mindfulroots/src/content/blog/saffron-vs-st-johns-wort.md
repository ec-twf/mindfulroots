Both saffron and St. John's Wort have genuine evidence for mild-to-moderate low mood, so the choice usually comes down to one thing: interactions. St. John's Wort interferes with a long list of medications — hormonal contraceptives, blood thinners, some heart and HIV drugs, and antidepressants — while saffron has far fewer known interactions. So if you take any regular medication, saffron is often the safer starting point; if you take none, St. John's Wort brings the deeper, older evidence base. Neither replaces professional care for moderate or severe depression. ## How they compare at a glance

St. John's Wort (*Hypericum perforatum*) and saffron (*Crocus sativus*) are both botanicals with a serotonergic story, but they differ most in the one place that matters for safety.

| Factor | Saffron | St. John's Wort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence base | Moderate; several RCTs | Well-studied; Cochrane-reviewed |
| Typical trial dose | 28–30 mg/day standardised | 300 mg 3×/day (0.3% hypericin) |
| Onset | ~4–8 weeks daily | ~4–8 weeks daily |
| Drug interactions | Few; caution with serotonergics | Many; broad enzyme induction |
| Best for | Mood support with fewer interaction risks | Mild–moderate low mood, no other meds |

## The evidence, honestly

St. John's Wort is the more established of the two. A [Cochrane review by Linde and colleagues (2008)](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD000448.pub3/full) pooled 29 trials and concluded that St. John's Wort extracts were superior to placebo and comparable to standard antidepressants in major depression of mild-to-moderate severity, with fewer side effects than the drugs in those studies. That is a large, well-scrutinised base.

Saffron's file is smaller but points the same way. A [meta-analysis by Hausenblas and colleagues (2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24299602/) found saffron outperformed placebo and rivalled antidepressants for mild-to-moderate depressive symptoms, and a [systematic review by Lopresti and Drummond (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25384672/) agreed while flagging the small samples and short durations. So on efficacy, the two are broadly in the same tier for mild-to-moderate low mood — St. John's Wort simply has more trials behind it.

## Why interactions usually decide it

This is where they part company. The [NCCIH profile of St. John's Wort](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/st-johns-wort) is blunt: it can weaken the effect of many important medications — including birth control pills, the blood thinner warfarin, some heart medications, transplant immunosuppressants, and certain HIV and cancer drugs — because it induces the liver enzymes that clear them. Combined with antidepressants, it can also push serotonin too high. Saffron carries a narrower caution: because it modulates serotonin, the [Memorial Sloan Kettering monograph on saffron](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/saffron) notes potential additive effects with serotonergic and antidepressant drugs, but it lacks St. John's Wort's broad enzyme-induction problem. If you take any regular medication, that difference is often the whole decision.

## What to look for in a standardised saffron extract supplement for low mood

If saffron is your pick, buy what the trials used: a **standardised extract**. Look for saffron standardised to its active compounds **crocin and safranal**, or a branded extract such as **affron**, with the dose printed per serving so you can match the **28–30 mg per day** used in studies rather than guessing. **Third-party testing** (NSF, USP, Informed Choice) earns its keep here because saffron is among the most adulterated botanicals sold, and a certificate is your check that you are getting real stigma extract. Favour a single-ingredient product over a crowded "mood blend" that hides how much saffron is actually inside. ## Cautions

Both are for support in mild low mood, not treatment of diagnosed depression. Do not combine either with a prescription antidepressant without your prescriber's guidance, and treat St. John's Wort as off-limits alongside the pill, blood thinners, or the other medications listed above unless a doctor or pharmacist has cleared it. Seek professional help if low mood is moderate, severe, or persistent.
