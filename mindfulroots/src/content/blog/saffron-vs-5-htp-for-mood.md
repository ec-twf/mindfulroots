If you are weighing saffron against 5-HTP for low mood, the short version is that saffron has the steadier human-trial record and the gentler safety profile, which makes it the easier first choice for most people. 5-HTP acts more directly on serotonin and appeals to people who want that mechanism, but it carries a real interaction risk with antidepressants and other serotonin-raising drugs. Both are support for mood, not a treatment for depression.

You can read the full evidence on each at our [saffron extract hub](/products/saffron-extract/) and our [5-HTP hub](/products/5-htp/). This post sets them side by side so you can see which fits your situation before you buy either.

## The verdict, before the detail

Choose **saffron** if you want the option with more consistent trials for low mood, a cleaner tolerability record, and fewer medication worries, particularly if you already take something for mood. Choose **5-HTP** if you and your prescriber have decided a direct serotonin precursor is worth trying and you have ruled out interactions. If you take any SSRI, SNRI, MAOI, triptan or other serotonergic medication, saffron is the lower-risk of the two to raise with your doctor, and 5-HTP is not something to start on your own.

## How each one works

Saffron (*Crocus sativus* stigma extract) is thought to support mood through several routes at once, including effects on serotonin reuptake and antioxidant activity, but it does so indirectly rather than by flooding the system with a precursor. 5-HTP (5-hydroxytryptophan), by contrast, is one chemical step away from serotonin: your body converts it into serotonin directly, which is both its appeal and the source of its interaction risk.

That mechanistic difference is the whole story behind their safety profiles. A gentle, multi-route nudge is harder to push too far than a direct precursor.

## What the evidence says

Saffron's edge is consistency. A meta-analysis of randomised trials concluded that saffron extract significantly outperformed placebo for symptoms of low mood in adults ([Hausenblas et al., *Journal of Integrative Medicine*, 2013](https://doi.org/10.3736/jintegrmed2013056)). Head-to-head work adds weight: a six-week randomised trial found saffron comparable to fluoxetine for mild-to-moderate low mood ([Noorbala et al., *Journal of Ethnopharmacology*, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2004.08.027)).

The picture for 5-HTP is thinner. A Cochrane review of 5-HTP and tryptophan for depression found that while some trials showed benefit over placebo, the quality of the evidence was low and the authors could not recommend it on that basis ([Shaw, Turner & Del Mar, *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews*, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003198)). A broader review of 5-HTP as a serotonin precursor reached a similar caution: plausible mechanism, limited robust trials ([Turner et al., *Pharmacology & Therapeutics*, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pharmthera.2005.06.004)). So saffron is not dramatically more powerful, it is simply better evidenced.

## Which saffron or 5-HTP supplement to buy for mood

The decision splits by product, not just by molecule. For either, a few criteria matter more than brand claims:

- **Saffron:** a standardised extract at the trial dose of roughly **28–30 mg per day**, with third-party testing. Saffron is among the most adulterated spices, so verified purity is not optional.
- **5-HTP:** a stated dose (commonly **50–100 mg**) from *Griffonia simplicifolia* seed extract, ideally paired with vitamin B6, the cofactor for the conversion step. Confirm there are no added serotonergic ingredients.
- **For both:** clear third-party testing and no proprietary blends that hide the dose.

Because the two overlap on mechanism, pick one rather than stacking them unsupervised. ## Cautions and interactions

This is where the two part ways most sharply. **5-HTP carries a serotonin-syndrome caution:** taken with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, triptans, tramadol, or other serotonergic drugs, it can contribute to a dangerous excess of serotonin activity. Do not combine 5-HTP with any such medication, and speak to your prescriber before starting it at all. Saffron should be avoided in pregnancy and at high doses, and anyone on mood medication should still check with a doctor first.

If low mood is persistent or severe, neither supplement is the answer on its own. Please see our [get help](/get-help/) page for proper support.
