Omega-3 fish oil is a supplement made from the flesh of oily fish, standardised for the long-chain fatty acids EPA and DHA that support brain, heart, and mood health. Cod liver oil is a different product from the same broad family: it is pressed from cod livers and, alongside EPA and DHA, naturally carries vitamins A and D. That extra vitamin content is the whole story of how these two differ. The [omega-3 fish oil page](/products/omega-3-fish-oil/) covers the concentrated forms, and the [low mood guide](/guides/low-mood/) sets omega-3s among other options.

## The verdict first

If your goal is a high, adjustable dose of EPA and DHA, for mood support, heart health, or triglycerides, standard fish oil is the cleaner choice because you can raise the dose without worrying about vitamins piling up. If you specifically want a modest omega-3 dose *plus* vitamins A and D in one capsule, cod liver oil is a reasonable all-in-one, as long as you keep an eye on the vitamin A total. Neither is a treatment for a diagnosed condition, and for most omega-3 goals they are not really rivals so much as tools for slightly different jobs.

## Head-to-head

| | Fish oil | Cod liver oil |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Flesh of oily fish | Cod liver |
| Main actives | EPA and DHA | EPA and DHA |
| Extra nutrients | Usually none | Vitamins A and D |
| EPA/DHA per serving | Often higher, concentrates available | Usually lower |
| Dose ceiling | Set by EPA/DHA goals | Capped by vitamin A |
| Best for | Higher, controllable omega-3 dose | Modest omega-3s plus A and D |

## Why the vitamins change the maths

The reason you cannot simply take more cod liver oil to match a concentrated fish oil is vitamin A. Cod liver oil is one of the richest natural sources of it, and the [NIH Office of Dietary Supplements vitamin A fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminA-HealthProfessional/) sets a tolerable upper intake level because chronic excess can harm the liver and, in pregnancy, the developing baby. So the vitamin A content, not the omega-3 content, sets your ceiling with cod liver oil. Fish oil has no such cap; its dose is governed only by your EPA and DHA target and general tolerance. Cod liver oil's vitamin D is a smaller consideration, though it still counts toward the [vitamin D fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/) limits if you also take a D supplement.

## For mood, EPA is the number to watch

When omega-3s are taken for mood, the evidence points to EPA-forward doses. A meta-analysis by [Liao and colleagues (2019)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31383846/) found supplements with a higher proportion of EPA were associated with better outcomes in depressive symptoms than DHA-dominant ones. The general omega-3 background is summarised in the [NIH ODS omega-3 fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Omega3FattyAcids-HealthProfessional/). The practical point is that reaching a studied EPA dose is easier with a concentrated fish oil, because cod liver oil's vitamin A ceiling gets in the way first.

## What to look for when choosing omega 3 fish oil or cod liver oil

Start with the EPA and DHA figures per serving, not the total "fish oil" weight, which includes other fats. For mood or heart goals, a concentrated fish oil delivering a clear, higher EPA and DHA amount is usually the better buy. If you choose cod liver oil, read the vitamin A per serving and add it to anything else you take, since that is your real limit. Either way, pick a product with third-party testing (IFOS, NSF, or USP) for purity and rancidity, since oxidised oil is a common quality problem, and check for a stated form (triglyceride or ethyl ester). The [omega-3 fish oil page](/products/omega-3-fish-oil/) lists the specs worth verifying before you buy.

## Cautions

Both oils are generally well tolerated, with fishy aftertaste, burping, and mild stomach upset the usual complaints. High doses of omega-3s can have a modest blood-thinning effect, so tell your doctor if you take anticoagulants or are due for surgery. Cod liver oil's real risk is vitamin A excess over time, which is why the label total matters, especially in pregnancy. If you take vitamin D or a multivitamin already, factor those in before adding cod liver oil.
