You should not add L-tryptophan to sertraline (Zoloft) on your own. L-tryptophan is a building block your body converts into serotonin, and sertraline already raises serotonin, so taking both can push serotonin activity too high. That is one of the recognised triggers for serotonin syndrome, a rare but potentially serious reaction. This is a genuine caution, not a "probably fine", and it needs your prescriber's input before anything else.

If your goal is calmer days or better sleep while staying on your medication, a supplement that does not act on serotonin is the safer conversation to have. You can see what to check on the [magnesium glycinate page](/products/magnesium-glycinate/), and the [sleep guide](/guides/sleep/) sets it among other supports.

## What L-tryptophan and sertraline each do

L-tryptophan is an essential amino acid. The body converts a portion of it into 5-HTP and then into serotonin, the neurotransmitter tied to mood, sleep, and appetite. Because of that conversion, supplemental L-tryptophan raises the raw material for serotonin production. Examine's [overview of tryptophan](https://examine.com/supplements/tryptophan/) describes this precursor role and notes the serotonergic caution when it is combined with drugs that also raise serotonin.

Sertraline is the generic name behind Zoloft, a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor. It works by keeping more serotonin available in the synapse. So both the drug and the supplement act on the same system, from different angles.

## Why the combination is a real caution

Serotonin syndrome happens when serotonin activity climbs too high, usually because two or more serotonergic agents are taken together. [Boyer and Shannon (2005)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15784664/) describe how it develops and why precursors and reuptake inhibitors are a higher-risk pairing than most people expect. L-tryptophan sits squarely in that higher-risk group, alongside 5-HTP and St John's wort, precisely because it feeds the serotonin pathway. The reaction is uncommon and often mild, but it can escalate, and the sensible response is to avoid stacking these without medical oversight rather than to test your own tolerance.

## A lower-risk option to discuss: magnesium glycinate

If you want support for calm or sleep while on sertraline, a supplement that works outside the serotonin pathway is the better place to start a prescriber conversation. Magnesium plays a role in the stress response and in normal nervous-system function, and it does not act as a serotonin precursor. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements [magnesium fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/) covers its role and safe intake ranges. The glycinate form is chosen for gentleness on the stomach. It has not been formally tested alongside sertraline, so it remains a prescriber discussion, but it does not carry the serotonin-stacking concern.

## What to look for in a magnesium glycinate supplement for calm while on an SSRI

Judge these products on specifics rather than calming claims. Check the elemental magnesium per serving; many people use 100 to 200 mg in the evening, and the label should state the elemental amount, not just the compound weight. Prefer the glycinate (bisglycinate) form for tolerability, and choose single-ingredient products so a sedative blend does not stack effects you cannot separate, which matters more when you are also on medication. Look for third-party testing from NSF or USP. The [magnesium glycinate page](/products/magnesium-glycinate/) checks these against verified specs, and the [sleep guide](/guides/sleep/) shows where a 150 mg elemental glycinate dose sits among other options. Whatever you choose, run it past your prescriber first.

## Cautions and the bottom line

If you have already taken L-tryptophan with sertraline and feel agitated or confused, notice a racing heart, sweating, shivering, muscle twitching, or a high temperature, treat it as a medical emergency and seek urgent care. Do not wait to see whether it settles.

Do not start, stop, or combine anything with your antidepressant without talking to your prescriber first. They know your full medication list and can weigh this up for you. If you are struggling with your mood, please use [get help](/get-help/).
