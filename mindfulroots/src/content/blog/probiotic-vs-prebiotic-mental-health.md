Probiotics and prebiotics are not competitors. A probiotic adds live bacteria to your gut; a prebiotic is the fermentable fibre that feeds the bacteria already living there. For mental health they work on the same gut-brain system from different ends, so the useful question is which to start with, not which one wins. If you want the more direct mood evidence, a strain-specific probiotic has it; prebiotic fibre is the cheaper foundation that supports the whole community.

## Two ends of the same system

The gut and brain talk to each other constantly, through the vagus nerve, immune signalling and compounds the gut bacteria produce. That conversation is why what happens in the gut can nudge mood at all. A probiotic tries to change the conversation by introducing helpful organisms directly. A prebiotic works upstream: it is a fibre, such as inulin or galacto-oligosaccharides, that humans cannot digest but gut bacteria can ferment, so it feeds and grows the community you already have. A product that combines both is a synbiotic, on the logic that live bacteria do better when you also send along their food.

## What the evidence actually shows

The idea that certain bacteria can influence mood is captured by the term "psychobiotics", proposed for strains that produce a mental-health benefit when consumed ([Dinan et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2013.05.001)). The most-cited human trial paired two strains, Lactobacillus helveticus R0052 and Bifidobacterium longum R0175, and reported lower psychological distress over 30 days ([Messaoudi et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114510004319)). On the prebiotic side, a trial found that a prebiotic reduced the waking cortisol response and shifted attention away from negative information ([Schmidt et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-014-3810-0)). Pulling it together, a systematic review and meta-analysis concluded that both probiotics and prebiotics show modest signals for depression and anxiety, while noting the studies are small and varied ([Liu et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2019.03.023)). For a plain-language overview of what is and is not established, the [NIH NCCIH probiotics page](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/probiotics-what-you-need-to-know) is a fair reference.

## Which probiotic or prebiotic supplement to buy for mood

For a probiotic, the label details matter more than the marketing. Look for named strains with their strain codes, not just a genus and species, because the mood research is strain-specific and "Lactobacillus" alone tells you little. Favour strains that have actually been studied for mood, such as Lactobacillus helveticus R0052 and Bifidobacterium longum R0175, and check for a stated CFU count and a use-by potency rather than a count at time of manufacture. For a prebiotic, look for a stated fibre type (inulin, GOS or FOS) and dose, and build up slowly since fermentable fibre can cause gas at first. Many people do best combining a strain-specific probiotic with more fibre from food. ## Cautions

Probiotics and prebiotics are generally safe for healthy adults, with the most common effect being temporary bloating or gas as the gut adjusts. People who are seriously immunocompromised, critically ill, or who have a central line should not take probiotics without medical advice, because of a small risk of infection. Gut-brain support is a complement to sleep, diet and movement, not a replacement for care of a diagnosed condition.
