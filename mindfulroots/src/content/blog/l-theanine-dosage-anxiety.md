L-theanine is an amino acid found almost exclusively in tea leaves, valued in supplements for producing a calm, focused state without sedation. The question most people arrive with is a practical one: how much should you actually take for anxiety? The short answer is that most human trials used 200 to 400 mg a day, often split across the day, with a single 200 mg dose serving as a reasonable acute option before a stressful event. The dose is not exotic, but matching it to your use case matters more than chasing a bigger number.

## The researched dose range

Two patterns of use show up in the literature. The first is acute: a single dose taken shortly before a stressor. Work by [Kimura and colleagues (2007)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16930802/) found that L-theanine blunted the physiological stress response, with effects seen at doses around 200 mg. The second pattern is daily use over weeks. A 2019 open-label study by [Hidese and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11102362) gave 200 mg a day for four weeks and reported improvements in stress-related symptoms and sleep quality.

A 2020 systematic review pulled these threads together and concluded that L-theanine, generally in the 200 to 400 mg range, may reduce stress and anxiety in people under acute or chronic pressure, while noting the evidence base is still modest ([Williams et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11130-019-00771-5)).

## How to split and time it

| Goal | Typical dose | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acute calm before a stressor | 200 mg | 30 to 60 min before |
| Steady daytime anxiety | 200 mg twice daily | Morning and afternoon |
| Sleep-onset support | 200 mg | 30 to 60 min before bed |

Starting low is the sensible default. Begin at 200 mg, see how you respond, and only move toward 400 mg a day if you need more. Splitting a daily dose keeps a steadier level and avoids loading it all at once.

## What to look for in an L-theanine supplement for anxiety dosing

Because the effect is dose-dependent, the label detail that matters most is a **clearly stated L-theanine amount per capsule**. A 100 mg or 200 mg capsule lets you split or stack cleanly toward the researched 200 to 400 mg range; a "calm blend" that hides the theanine dose inside a proprietary mix does not. Prefer the **patented Suntheanine form** where possible, since it is the pure L-isomer used in much of the research, and confirm the product is **third-party tested** (USP, NSF, or an independent certificate of analysis). Check that there are **no unlisted stimulants** if your goal is calm rather than energy. ## Cautions and interactions

L-theanine is well tolerated across the studied range, and side effects are uncommon. The main considerations are practical rather than dangerous: it can add to the effect of sedatives and blood-pressure medication, so combine those cautiously, and there is limited data on use during pregnancy. L-theanine supports a calmer stress response; it does not treat a diagnosed anxiety disorder, and persistent or severe anxiety deserves a clinician's assessment rather than a higher dose. If you take prescription medication, check with your prescriber before starting.
