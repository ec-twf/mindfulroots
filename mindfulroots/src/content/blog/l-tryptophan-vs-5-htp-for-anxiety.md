If your interest is calm and you want the gentler, more self-limiting option, L-tryptophan is the more forgiving starting point because the body controls how much of it becomes serotonin. If you want a more direct effect and you are careful with dose, 5-HTP acts closer to the end of the pathway and tends to work sooner. Neither has a strong evidence base specifically for anxiety, so this is less about a winner and more about matching the route to how cautious you want to be. Both feed the same serotonin system, and both carry the same hard safety rule below.

Before anything else: if you take any antidepressant or other serotonergic medication, neither L-tryptophan nor 5-HTP is an option for you without a prescriber's sign-off. More on that in a moment. ## How each one works

L-tryptophan is an essential amino acid you get from food, and it is the raw material at the top of the serotonin pathway. Your body converts it first into 5-HTP, then into serotonin. That first conversion is rate-limited, which means the body only makes as much as its enzymes allow, so tryptophan tends to produce a slower, buffered rise. Tryptophan also competes with other large amino acids to cross into the brain, so a protein-heavy meal can blunt its effect.

5-HTP (5-hydroxytryptophan), usually extracted from *Griffonia simplicifolia* seed, sits one step further along. It skips the rate-limited step, crosses the blood-brain barrier without fighting for transport, and converts more readily into serotonin. That directness is its appeal and also its catch: it is easier to push serotonin higher than you intended. A [2006 review by Turner, Loftis and Blackwell](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16023217/) lays out this precursor-loading logic and its limits in plain terms.

## What the evidence actually says for anxiety

This is where honesty matters more than marketing. Most of the research on both supplements targets low mood and sleep, not anxiety as a primary outcome. A [2002 Cochrane review by Shaw, Turner and Del Mar](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD003198/full) found that 5-HTP and tryptophan beat placebo for depression, while flagging that the trials were small and of modest quality. For anxiety specifically, the direct evidence is thinner. Work on tryptophan depletion shows that lowering serotonin availability can worsen mood and irritability in vulnerable people, as summarised in a [2015 review by Lindseth and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25858202/), which supports the pathway's relevance without proving either supplement treats anxiety. The independent research summaries at [Examine's 5-HTP page](https://examine.com/supplements/5-htp/) reach a similar cautious read. Treat both as mood and sleep support that may indirectly ease tension, not as anxiety remedies.

## Safety: the rule that binds both

Both supplements raise serotonin, so both carry the same serious caution. Never combine L-tryptophan or 5-HTP with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, triptans, tramadol, dextromethorphan, or St John's wort. Doing so risks serotonin syndrome, a potentially dangerous excess of serotonin activity that can cause agitation, sweating, tremor, a racing heart, and confusion. This is not a mild caution to note and move past. If you take any medication that touches serotonin, do not start either supplement without your prescriber's explicit approval. Long-term safety data for both are limited, so any ongoing use is best kept under a clinician's eye.
